活用表 Verb Conjugation

Every N5 and N4 verb form for all verb types: plain and polite, present and past, positive and negative, plus て-form, potential, passive, causative, conditionals and commands. Japanese has two tenses: non-past (present and future) and past.

How each form is built

う-verbs (godan) shift the last kana along its row: あ, い, え, お. る-verbs (ichidan) drop る and add the ending. する and 来る are irregular.

行く→ 行って the one exception
  • た-form = て-form with て→た and で→だ: 書いて → 書いた, 読んで → 読んだ.
  • る-verbs end in -iru or -eru (見る, 食べる). Some look the same but are う-verbs: 帰る, 入る, 走る, 知る, 切る, 要る.
  • Verbs ending in う use わ for the negative, passive and causative: 買わない, not 買あない.
  • ある is irregular in the negative: ない, not あらない.
  • In casual speech, る-verb potentials often drop ら: 食べれる, 見れる.
  • Causative-passive of う-verbs is usually shortened: 書かされる (from 書かせられる). Verbs ending in す keep the long form: 話させられる.
